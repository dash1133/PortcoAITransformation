# YouTube listing — PortCo film

Written against Hormozi's title logic: **specificity over cleverness**, signal the
**Value Equation** (dream outcome up, perceived likelihood up, time and effort
down), **target the avatar so precisely the wrong viewer scrolls past**, and open
a **curiosity gap the video actually closes** — a title the film doesn't pay off
kills retention, and retention is what the algorithm scores.

Every number below already appears in the film. The client stays masked and
neither the consulting firm nor the service business is named.

---

## Title — recommended

> **Your Portfolio Company Doesn't Need Another AI Vendor. It Needs an Asset It Owns.**

81 characters. Contrarian framing plus avatar identity. It is also the film's
actual thesis, so the click is paid off in the first 45 seconds. This is the one
built to be *shared* — operating partners forward opinions they agree with, not
vendor credentials.

## Optional stronger hook — your call

An opening line built on the MIT finding that ~95% of enterprise GenAI pilots
show no measurable P&L impact would hit harder than the proof line, and it is
third-party published research rather than our claim. I left it out because
**the film never says it**, and Hormozi's own constraint applies: a title or
hook the video doesn't pay off costs more in retention than it gains in clicks.

If you want it, the honest way in is to attribute it and keep it in the
description body rather than the title:

> *Research out of MIT put it bluntly last year: roughly 95% of enterprise GenAI
> pilots produce no measurable P&L impact. That is the problem this film is
> about.*

Worth deciding deliberately — it is the single biggest available lever on
click-through, and the single easiest way to over-promise.

## Where the actual segment spread comes from

A four-minute capabilities film is not a viral-shaped asset, and no title fixes
that. "Viral within our segment" realistically means a high share rate among a
few thousand operating partners — and in this audience that sharing happens on
LinkedIn, in vertical clips, not on YouTube.

The four moments worth cutting down, in order of strength:

| Clip | Source | Why it travels |
|---|---|---|
| **$1.5M, month 3 of 24** | 2:16–3:05 | A real number with a real clock on it. The only proof in the film that a stranger can't dismiss as positioning. |
| **The four things** | 0:22–0:44 | Recognition. This is the "that's us" moment, and recognition is what gets forwarded. |
| **Not another vendor's platform** | 0:44–1:01 | The thesis, standalone. Pairs with title #1. |
| **What you own vs. what you rent** | 1:31–2:16 | The diagram does the work; strong even muted, which matters on a silent autoplay feed. |

YouTube is where these land after the clip does the recruiting — so the
description above is written to convert someone who arrived already interested,
not to win a cold impression.
