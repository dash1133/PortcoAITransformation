## A · The case study — recommended

```
Three months ago we started an AI program with a heavy-equipment service business.

We didn't start with a chatbot. We mapped their top five processes, then built one Enterprise AI Core underneath all of them — connectors into their ERP, CRM and OEM warranty portals, their warranty policy knowledge, their claim and appeal templates.

Month three of twenty-four. One workflow is live. $1.5M of stuck warranty claims is now visible and being worked.

That isn't the number I care about.

I care that the next four workflows will each land faster than the first — because the Core is already built. That's the difference between a project and an asset. An asset compounds.

Most AI money inside a portfolio company buys pilots. Pilots don't show up at exit.

A platform the company owns does.

Four minutes on how it gets built.

How Portfolio Companies Turn AI Into Exit Value.
```

## One thing to hold the line on

The film says the $1.5M is **"now visible and being worked"** — not recovered,
not collected. That distinction is the difference between a claim that survives
diligence and one that doesn't. If this number gets repeated in a deck, a
comment reply, or a sales conversation, it should keep those words.
