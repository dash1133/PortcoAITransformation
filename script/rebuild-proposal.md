diligence. That is what makes the film urgent instead of aspirational.

## 4 · THE THREE QUESTIONS — NEW · dark · ~32s

The sharpest scene in the film. Three numbered questions, revealed one at a
time, then the consequence.

**On screen**
- Eyebrow: WHEN THE ASSET GOES TO MARKET
- Headline: *Diligence asks three questions*
- 01 — What was the cost base before? *Who signed off on it?*
- 02 — What is in the P&L today? *Versus identified. Versus run-rate.*
- 03 — If the vendor, the team or the model changes — *what survives?*
- Footer rule, orange: *What cannot be substantiated comes out of the price.*

**VO** — `[serious]`
> When the asset goes to market, diligence asks three questions. What was the
> cost base before, and who signed off on it. What is in the P&L today, as
> opposed to identified, or run-rate. And if the vendor contract, the team, or
> the model changes … what survives. Most sellers cannot answer all three.
> `[confident]` What cannot be substantiated comes out of the price. Sometimes
> it comes out after close.

## 5 · TWO KINDS OF EBITDA — NEW · light · ~30s

The centrepiece. A two-column split, deliberately unequal in visual weight —
the right column gets the colour and the space.

**On screen**

| EFFICIENCY EBITDA | CAPABILITY EBITDA |
|---|---|
| Same work. Fewer hours. | Work you could not do before. |
| Your competitor buys the same tools next quarter. | Quote in hours, not days. |
| | Serve accounts that were never economic. |
| | Absorb volume without adding headcount. |
| **A cost reset** (grey) | **A multiple** (orange) |

Kicker line under both: *Both lift EBITDA in year one.*

**VO** — `[thoughtful]`
> And not all AI earnings are worth the same multiple. Efficiency EBITDA is the
> same work with fewer hours. The savings are real … but if your competitor can
> buy the same tools next quarter, a buyer treats it as a cost reset.
> `[confident]` Capability EBITDA is work the company could not do before.
> Quoting in hours instead of days. Serving accounts that were never economic to
> serve. Taking on volume without adding headcount. Both lift EBITDA in year
> one. Only one of them changes what the company is worth.
